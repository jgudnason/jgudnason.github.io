## Writing

I have spent the last few weeks writing. Most academics feel they should write more, so this has
been a good and guilt-free period. We submitted one journal paper a couple of weeks ago and are
well on the way to a full draft of a second. Both are on automatic speech recognition for
conversational speech, and I hope to describe them in more detail once they are accepted and
published.

Writing is also a chance to take stock of the field and re-evaluate some of your own older work.
Doing so, I want to acknowledge two co-authors who led that work.

## Open speech recognition for Icelandic

Anna Björk Nikulásdóttir led the first open-source speech recognition work in our lab and in
Iceland. She is first author of [Open ASR for Icelandic: Resources and a Baseline
System](https://aclanthology.org/L18-1495/), presented at the Language Resources and Evaluation
Conference in 2018. Rereading it reminded me how much later work rests on it. The paper is
thorough, covering the resources, a complete recognition system and its evaluation, and the system
compared well with the state of the art at the time.

## Speech recognition for parliament

Inga Rún Helgadóttir developed a more specialised system for Althingi, the Icelandic parliament,
described in [Building an ASR Corpus Using Althingi's Parliamentary
Speeches](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2017-903) at Interspeech 2017 and [The Althingi ASR
System](https://doi.org/10.21437/Interspeech.2019-1248) at Interspeech 2019. Aligning a large
amount of parliamentary audio with its text, normalising that text, choosing the modelling
approach and evaluating the result were each substantial pieces of work. The outcome was a system
that, I believe, is still used at Althingi to produce transcripts of parliamentary speeches.

Anna and Inga co-authored each other's papers, and Matthías Pétursson, Róbert Kjaran, Michal
Borský and Judy Y. Fong were co-authors on one or more of them, as was I.
